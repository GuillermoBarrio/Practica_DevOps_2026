Before the European Bell: A Packed Macro Week Moves Into View
By Neil Campling

European equities are heading for a muted start to the week, but elsewhere there’s plenty keeping traders busy, from carry trades and dollar shorts to bond “tourists” and a flood of AI paper.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures were 0.1% lower at 7:25 a.m. CET, while contracts on the FTSE 100 were flat. 
Asian stocks are lower, with Hong Kong weaker as investors digest a large cash call from Alibaba. There’s also profit taking on the Kospi as heavyweight Samsung fell as much as 9.4% as a shareholder return plan underwhelmed. Futures point to losses in the US later. 
Meanwhile, oil is ticking lower with some barrels managing to move out of the Strait of Hormuz late Friday, while details of US economic measures against Iran expected later today could shape the next directional move. 
Markets at a Glance:
 
Source: Bloomberg
There are plenty of catalysts on the horizon for investors this week including Nvidia earnings, the Fed’s preferred PCE inflation gauge, Bessent’s potential new fiscal plan and the Jackson Hole meeting in the US. Closer to home, we get the German key Ifo indicator, French consumer confidence and flash inflation numbers for Spain and France. 
Speaking of inflation, the costs of the AI buildout are set to get more expensive as prices of servers with Nvidia chips are said to be getting a 15% price hike. Elsewhere, the financing of data center projects are increasingly drawing interest from junk bond “tourists.” 
Key questions for the week ahead, as posed in the Week in Review, include: Does $90 oil keep the rate-hike trade alive? Is this still a pullback within an uptrend, or the start of a broader de-risking? Is JD Sports an isolated warning, or the start of a softer consumer tape?
 
In deals, Banca Generali is evaluating the unsolicited bid from Monte Paschi, while Partners Group will exit its private credit investment in Gong cha after Bain Capital bought the bubble-tea brand. Shell’s US chemical assets are said to have drawn interest from potential bidders including ExxonMobil and LyondellBasell, the FT reports.    
In other assets, hedge funds have slashed short-only positions on European diesel to the lowest in over two years. And Russia is considering grain purchases to offset export disruptions.  
In geopolitics, Ukraine offered Russia a truce on attacks against ships carrying agricultural commodities through the Black Sea, but Moscow rejected the deal. Italy’s government extended a tax cut on diesel fuel until Wednesday. Canada will apply counter-tariffs on $20 billion of US products on Sept. 8, escalating the feud rocking one of the world’s biggest trading relationships.
Bloomberg Intelligence strategist Laurent Douillet notes European 10-year bond yields have risen 40bp since the end of June, pushing lower equity-duration sectors (energy, financials and materials) toward the top of the return table. The move is driven primarily by a higher term premium (34 bp), which is weighing disproportionately on companies with more back-end-loaded earnings.
Citi strategist Beata Manthey highlights that the long-term case for holding European equities in global portfolios may be improving. Alongside the potential for a continued cyclical uplift, market support comes from idiosyncratic tailwinds including fiscal stimulus and the region’s emerging role as an AI diversifier, Manthey adds.
Lastly, Europe’s luxury giants are seeing signs of green shoots in the key Chinese market, leading to a pickup in earnings estimates for the sector.
