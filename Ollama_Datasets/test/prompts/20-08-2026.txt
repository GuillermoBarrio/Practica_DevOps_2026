Before the European Bell: Stocks Can’t Shake the Losing Streak
By Farah Elbahrawy

(Bloomberg) -- (Click here or type SQUA <GO> to listen to Bloomberg’s new EMEA Equities Squawk) 
European stocks look set to extend their slow summer grind lower as investors await fresh catalysts, with much of the region’s good news already priced in.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures were 0.1% lower at 7:28 a.m. CET, while FTSE 100 futures were flat. US stock futures were higher after a mixed session as the Treasury unveiled plans to buy back longer-maturity debt, while Fed minutes showed officials favored a rate hike last month amid a “highly uncertain” inflation outlook.
Markets at a Glance: 
 
Source: Bloomberg
In Asia, stocks are rallying with investors rewarding memory chipmakers after SK Hynix unveiled plans for a record buyback. A report that Samsung Electronics is planning to announce a shareholder return plan topping 100 trillion won ($72 billion) fueled further optimism. 
Any good news for the semiconductor space will be welcomed by those with long positions. It’s been a volatile year for the crowded trade, which hit a record high in June before tumbling on concerns about overspending on artificial intelligence. An attempted rebound has since stalled, derailed by rising bond yields. The focus will now turn to Nvidia’s earnings next week for signs of whether AI demand has further room to grow.
 
The rally in European stocks has also faded in August with lower volumes and a lack of any positive news to reignite the gains. The Stoxx 600 has dropped for six days, the longest losing streak since September 2023. 
 
UK bank stocks may be in focus as the Green Party has said it wants to apply a 38% windfall tax on lenders, adding to the pressure from the left on Prime Minister Andy Burnham to target the City of London. Second-quarter profits at HSBC increased 60% to $10.1 billion, and Barclays, Natwest and Lloyds posted cumulative profits in the first-half of almost £30 billion. Their shares have significantly outperformed the FTSE 100 over the past two years.
 
On the geopolitical front, Trump announced plans to inflict what he called “an ECONOMIC D-DAY” against Iran. Trump didn’t specify what measures he would take but his threat to target Iran’s trading partners, none of which he cited by name, put an immediate focus on China, which buys the bulk of Iran’s oil. Brent crude held above $91 a barrel.
Meanwhile, China’s relationship with the European Union is in focus as it’s using new legal powers to block EU scrutiny of alleged state support for its companies, adding friction ahead of an October deadline to resolve outstanding trade disputes.
