European stocks look set for modest gains at the open, tracking some late-session strength in the US, albeit on the second-lowest trading volumes of the year.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures were 0.3% higher at 7:25 a.m. CET, while contracts on the FTSE 100 were up 0.1%. 
Asian stocks are steady, as the AI trade continues to disperse. South Korea initially reflected the sharp drop in the US memory names Monday, while Japan is a notable out-performer with banks among the gainers. Futures point to gains in the US later. 
Markets at a Glance: 
 
Source: Bloomberg
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Monday promised an “economic onslaught against Iran’s financial connections around the globe,” risking a collision course with its main trading partner, China. The speech gave few details and Bessent conceded it was more of a warning shot. 
Dollar debasement trades remain in focus across the globe. US Treasury buybacks are still a hot topic despite Bessent stopping short of announcing any new bond measures. Gold climbed to the highest in more than three months, Bitcoin touched intraday levels not reached since mid-May and carry trades for both G10 and emerging markets strengthened.
 
A number of Wall Street strategists say Treasury buybacks will do little to cut long rates and High Frequency Economics caution the action may be a test to “seize control of monetary conditions” away from the Fed. Stan Druckenmiller, Bessent’s early mentor, wrote “governments defending prices against fundamentals always lose” in an opinion column in the Wall Street Journal.
Closer to home, macro data is in focus today, with the Ifo survey of German firms set to shed light on whether the country’s struggling industry can stage a rebound this year. Stronger-than-expected GDP growth in the first half of 2026 showed Germany was relatively resilient to higher energy prices, prompting Bloomberg Economics to raise 2026 growth forecast to 0.9% from 0.5%. French consumer confidence data for August will show if repeated heat waves and higher pump prices are taking a more persistent toll on consumers.
Positioning in Europe remains the strongest on aggregate among developed markets despite a modest deterioration in sentiment, according to Citigroup strategists including David Chew. Recent flows were driven mainly by new short bets in the Euro Stoxx 50 and the DAX, while European banks and the FTSE 100 witnessed increased long unwinds. Overall, positioning is still moderately bullish. The chief market strategist at Man Group, Kristina Hooper, notes Europe is in a bright spot underscored by the positive impact of defense spending. 
Analyst rating changes include a DKSH downgrade to hold at Berenberg, who cited risk-reward as no longer being compelling, and guidance that is increasingly reliant on flawless 2H execution. Citi raised its recommendation on Next to buy from neutral, while Drax was raised to neutral from sell at UBS. Jupiter was initiated with a buy rating at Berenberg, which said the British wealth manager is primed to outperform when a sustained improvement in investor sentiment occurs.
Meanwhile, Ireland’s government received yet another warning from its fiscal watchdog about not overspending when the economy is already strong as it risks stoking inflation. Elsewhere, Hungary’s central bank is poised to deliver its third consecutive interest rate cut on Tuesday after the inflation rate fell to the lowest level in a decade. 
UK power equipment firm Aggreko filed for a US IPO which could value the company at about $15 billion. Volkswagen may be active later with the CEO due to address staff at the company’s Wolfsburg plant in the first of a series of townhall gatherings seeking to explain sweeping cost-cutting plans.
