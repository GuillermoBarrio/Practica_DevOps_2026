Subject: Before the European Bell: AI Bulls Get Reassurance They Wanted
Before the European Bell: AI Bulls Get Reassurance They Wanted
By Farah Elbahrawy and Neil Campling
08/27/2026 01:32:49 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- Any lingering doubts about the AI-fueled rally have been swept aside by blockbuster earnings from the company at the heart of it all: Nvidia. European stock futures are following US peers higher, putting markets on both sides of the Atlantic on course for a positive session.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures climbed 0.4% at 7:25 a.m. CET, while FTSE 100 futures underperformed, falling 0.4%. Contracts on the Nasdaq 100 jumped 0.6% while those on the S&P 500 futures gained 0.3%. Asian stocks are rising for a third day.
Markets at a Glance: 
 
Bloomberg
Nvidia gave a bullish sales outlook, expecting revenue to grow around 70% in fiscal 2028, easing concerns about both fading AI spending and a bubble in the sector. The company also said its deals are not circular, addressing investor concerns that its web of investment pacts with firms across the artificial intelligence ecosystem could leave the industry on shakier ground. 
 
Its shares, which have trailed key AI trades over the past year, rose over 4% in late trading. Citigroup analysis showed technology remains among the three most crowded sectors globally. Within the Magnificent Seven, however, the picture is more mixed: Alphabet and Meta remain crowded longs, while Nvidia is moving away from that quadrant.
Salesforce was another positive for the AI trade. The software company gave an outlook for strong revenue expansion and deepened its partnership with Anthropic PBC, reassuring investors that it can compete successfully in the AI era. The news is a relief for the software sector, which has been battered this year at the expense of perceived AI winners. There have been signs of a rebound, with the group outperforming semiconductors for the second month in a row. 
 
The same Citigroup data showed technology is the most crowded trade on a cap-weighted basis in Europe, while financials are the most crowded on an equal-weight basis. Energy sector crowding had the largest decline in August after a July spike on geopolitical tensions.
On that front, Brent crude prices fell for a fourth day on Thursday to $87.43 a barrel as traders weighed progress on increasing energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz against rising Russia-Ukraine tensions that are curbing output. Russia is said to be preparing to escalate attacks on Ukraine after concluding that negotiations for a peace deal have reached a dead end.
Meanwhile, central bankers from around the world will begin their meetings at the Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium on Thursday, before all eyes turn to Kevin Warsh for his first speech at the event the following day.
Among key corporate news in Europe, Swedish mining group Boliden has agreed to acquire Votorantim’s stake in Nexa Resources, taking control of the zinc and silver producer and expanding its operations into Brazil and Peru. HSBC is said to be planning to overhaul its Singapore operations by combining its major banking services under one entity to streamline the structure.
