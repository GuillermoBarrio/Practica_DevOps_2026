Before the European Bell: On Brink of Worst Run Since 2016
By Farah Elbahrawy
08/21/2026 01:36:50 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- (Click here or type SQUA <GO> to listen to Bloomberg’s new EMEA Equities Squawk) 
Set for a muted end to the week, European stocks are on the brink of an unwelcome milestone: their longest losing streak in a decade. A flurry of economic data could offer some relief, while regional equity funds continue to attract inflows.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures and contracts on the FTSE 100 were flat as of 7:25 a.m. CET. Asian stocks rose, with Samsung Electronics’ plans to return as much as $79 billion to shareholders in focus.
Markets at a Glance:
 
Source: Bloomberg
In the US, futures were muted after a negative session. The focus is currently on Anthropic PBC, which is said to be expecting to match or beat the size of SpaceX’s record-setting initial public offering. 
The flood of capital shows little sign of abating, with Broadcom said to be in talks with a group of lenders to raise more than $60 billion in debt for an AI chip financing deal that will benefit Anthropic PBC and other companies.
Over in Europe, the Stoxx 600 is headed for a second week of losses, with the index falling for seven straight sessions. If a negative session on Friday extends the pattern, the index will mark its longest losing run since November 2016. Still, the picture is not all grim  — the benchmark is less than 2% below its record high from earlier this month.
 
Despite the shaky trading, flows to European equity funds stayed positive at $200 million in the week through August 19, mostly attributed to strong inflows to regional funds, but offset by outflows from UK funds, according to a Citigroup note citing EPFR Global data.
There’s a slew of economic data due later Friday, including PMI prints for the UK, France and Germany. The readings for the euro zone are predicted to stay in growth territory. Data released earlier showed British consumers are more confident than at any point since Labour took power two years ago, further evidence that the national mood has improved since Andy Burnham took over as prime minister in July.
An analysis by HSBC strategists shows AI is the dominant narrative for European equities, as well as infrastructure capex, corporate margin resilience, and the ECB policy path. FX volatility and consumers trading down are emerging narratives, a team led by Duncan Toms wrote in a note. “As for the significant sector narratives, we find a defence order backlog as relevant for Industrials, and grid expansion for Utilities, among others.”
In key corporate news, Holcim is acquiring part of the European business of James Hardie Industries Plc for €840 million ($982 million), expanding its building, sustainable flooring and walling solution offerings. Meanwhile, Deutsche Bank is making a major push to rebuild its energy trading business more than a decade after it largely exited the commodities sector.
Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena is seeking to buy two separate banks for a combined value of €34 billion ($40 billion) as it seeks to prevent being bought by rival Intesa Sanpaolo. The lender has launched an all-share bid for Banco BPM valuing it at €25.3 billion and another one for Banca Generali valuing it €8.7 billion.  
Paschi has “gone from defense to double-or-nothing”, notes Nicolas Marmurek, co-head of special situations at Square Global Markets, in a “bold countermove which looks like a very complicated way of saying “not Intesa.”
