Subject: Before the European Bell: Ebbing Oil and Rate Relief Set Tone
Before the European Bell: Ebbing Oil and Rate Relief Set Tone
By Levin Stamm
08/26/2026 01:35:02 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- (Click here or type SQUA <GO> to listen to Bloomberg’s new EMEA Equities Squawk) 
European stocks are set for slight gains at the open, propelled by falling oil prices as well as signs that US action to tame borrowing costs are bearing fruit.
Euro Stoxx 50 and FTSE 100 futures were both 0.1% higher at 7:25 a.m. CET. Brent crude prices are down for a third consecutive day, extending this week’s decline to about 8% to trade at $86.71 per barrel, after Iran and Oman discussed an “interim framework” aimed at resuming shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.
Markets at a Glance: 
 
Source: Bloomberg
Chipmakers led gains in Asia ahead of US behemoth Nvidia’s earnings, which are due after the market close on Wednesday. The company is estimated to report revenue that doubled from a year earlier. On Tuesday, it snapped a seven-day streak of losses as investors took profits ahead of the print.
With the earnings season drawing to a close, corporate action is back in the spotlight. Banco BPM said the price offered by rival Monte Paschi was pretty much the same as its market valuation. And Siemens Energy is preparing to separate its Transformation of Industry division to streamline its business and refine allocation of resources.
In economic data, the US government is set to release July’s reading of the personal consumption expenditures price index at 2:30 p.m. CET — the Federal Reserve’s preferred inflation gauge — ahead of Kevin Warsh’s much-awaited Jackson Hole speech later in the week. Economists estimate the PCE price index rose 3.6% from a year ago. The same report is forecast to show little change in inflation-adjusted personal spending.
In other asset classes, Treasuries have outperformed equivalent-maturity swaps, narrowing the 30-year spread between the two to the smallest since February. Benchmark US yields have also drifted lower after initially see-sawing in the wake of the Scott Bessent‘s plan to “at least double” its buybacks of longer-dated bonds. That’s also putting brakes on the debasement trade, with gold pulling back slightly from a three-month high.
Back in Europe Wednesday, European Central Bank Executive Board member Isabel Schnabel said interest rates must rise further as the lengthy conflict in the Middle East and surprisingly strong euro-zone economy pose upside risks to inflation.
 
France is also set to move back into focus, with the first of a string of reviews by credit assessors expected this Friday. Uncertainty on whether a solution for the country’s debt will be found as well as next year’s presidential election have already weighed on sentiment, with the CAC 40 Index trailing the region’s other major benchmarks.
Meanwhile, UBS strategists including Sutanya Chedda say the recovery in European small and mid-cap stocks is gaining traction, as their macro model now puts the probability of economic expansion at 64.5% and earnings revisions turn positive. Still, they caution the rebound remains uneven. Larger companies are seeing stronger earnings upgrades, financing costs are weighing on cash generation and investor flows into small and mid-caps remain negative, they add, favoring selective stock-picking over a broad bet on the asset class.
 
In analyst rating changes, software firm SAP and infrastructure company Ferrovial were cut to neutral at UBS, while Denmark’s Orsted was raised to buy at Citi.
After the close Tuesday, EasyJet and Ithaca Energy were indicated to join the FTSE 100, according to a statement from FTSE Russell. Luxury carmaker Aston Martin Lagonda is set to leave the FTSE 250, a move that would cement its status as a small-cap stock and highlight its faltering turnaround.
