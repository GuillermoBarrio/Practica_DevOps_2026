Before the European Bell: The Make-or-Break Earnings Week
By Neil Campling
07/27/2026 01:34:22 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- Stocks are poised to begin the week on the front foot after a quieter weekend on the geopolitical front. The focus now shifts to a packed earnings calendar and a string of key macroeconomic events.
A slew of earnings from Europe and the US, interest-rate decisions from the Federal Reserve, Bank of Japan and Bank of England, euro-area GDP data and fresh clues on the pace of AI infrastructure investment will keep traders on their toes.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures are 0.9% higher at 7:25 a.m. CET, while contracts on the energy-heavy FTSE 100 are up a more modest 0.3% with Brent crude down by over 3% since Europe went home Friday. Asia is more mixed with the tech-heavy indexes in South Korea and Taiwan reflecting a weak handover from Nasdaq 100 losses on Friday. And futures are pointing to gains in the US later.
The scorecard for earnings so far, with roughly 30% of the Stoxx 600 index through results, shows blended EPS growth is tracking about 17% year-on-year while sales beats have been relatively broad. Earnings are helping to shape which companies escape the macro drag, a theme explored in the Stoxx 600 Week in Review column. 
 
Bloomberg Strategist Ruhell Amin poses three questions for the week ahead: Can the next earnings wave broaden the winners’ list? Can banks keep outperforming as the rate-trade matures? Will the macro data validate the cyclical trade?
Later this week, officials in the euro zone will get an initial batch of data to help them gauge if they should deliver another rate hike. GDP likely rebounded with a 0.2% increase in the second quarter, while inflation likely picked up to 2.9% in July, according to the median forecasts of economists. Those numbers are scheduled for Thursday and Friday, respectively. ECB Governing Council member Ante Zigman said the fallout from renewed fighting in the Middle East will take some time to show up in the data. 
 
A surge in a handful of stocks has made the Stoxx 600 Index the most concentrated it’s been in years based on the number of firms driving the gains, data compiled by Bloomberg show. Almost 60% of the benchmark’s 8.8% advance this year has come from just 10 stocks. ASML alone has contributed nearly a quarter of the gains, followed by HSBC at 8%.
 
In other assets, oil and natural gas sank after the US paused strikes against Iran, easing tensions even as the Houthis claimed attacks against Saudi targets. Fuel demand is outstripping supply, especially for diesel, and refineries are running at full capacity, risking outages and equipment breakdowns.
In politics, Chancellor Friedrich Merz replaced Germany’s transportation minister after a failed first attempt, seeking to stay on track with a revamp of his government ahead of several regional elections in September. New UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham will meet Volodymyr Zelenskyy today, hosting his first international visitor since becoming prime minister. 
In deals, UniCredit CEO Andrea Orcel said he sees room for an agreement over the bank’s proposed takeover of Commerzbank, signaling confidence that talks with the German government and other stakeholders can lead to a deal as discussions between the two sides are set to resume. 
Japan’s Seven & i has ended talks over a potential investment in Zabka, Poland’s largest convenience store operator. Meanwhile, Argenx agreed to acquire Forte Biosciences at $77 per share in cash, representing a total equity value of approximately $2.2 billion. Boards of both companies have approved the deal.
