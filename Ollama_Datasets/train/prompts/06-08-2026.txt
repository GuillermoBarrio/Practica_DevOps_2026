Subject: Before the European Bell: Futures Gain on Hormuz Hopes
Before the European Bell: Futures Gain on Hormuz Hopes
By Neil Campling and Julien Ponthus
08/06/2026 01:37:49 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- European futures point to further gains at the open, buoyed by optimism over a potential transit route agreement for the Strait of Hormuz. Traders have one eye on the last major day of Europe’s earnings season, and a slate of macro data. 
Euro Stoxx 50 futures are 0.2% higher at 7:28 a.m. CET after the underlying benchmark closed at another record the prior session, while FTSE 100 futures are up 0.1%. 
In Asia, tech stocks are weighing on the MSCI Asia Pacific benchmark, as reactions to Western Digital and Sandisk earnings reflect concerns about the storage and memory chip market. 
 
Source: Bloomberg
Still supportive for the equity setup in Europe is the economic momentum indicator in the Eurozone outpacing the US and breadth of EPS beats. That’s as Banco BPM and Technoprobe reported upside surprise on headline results since the close on Wednesday. 
Over 30 Stoxx 600 Index members are due to report Thursday, with Diageo, Siemens and Deutsche Telekom all on deck. The latter raised its share buy-back program by up to €3 billion. Earlier this week a big options trade crossed in the form of a put spread collar, for more color see the EMEA Options Snapshot. 
 
With M&A in focus in the banking sector, arbs will likely be listening in to Commerzbank’s earnings conference call at 9 a.m CET, after UniCredit’s gradual takeover of the German bank cleared a key regulatory hurdle earlier this week. 
And Italy’s banking consolidation saga took another turn after Banco BPM’s CEO said the lender would consider a tie-up with its largest shareholder, Credit Agricole. That follows BPM’s decision to abandon a merger plan with Monte dei Paschi, and reports that Paschi is potentially considering a bid for BPM, even as Paschi remains a target of Intesa Sanpaolo. 
 
On the macro front, Eurozone retail sales, the ECB economic bulletin, German factory orders, industrial production in Italy and Spain are expected. Poland is analyzing how to tax “huge” windfall profits of oil companies after a presidential decision to block the previous bill. And the UK government will allow over 100 companies, including AstraZeneca and JLR, to bring in workers on a fast-track visa.  
In other assets, bond and currency investors are debating if it’s time to dust off last year’s ‘Sell America’ trade after a flurry of economic-policy decisions out of Washington. Sovereign wealth fund Kuwait Investment Authority is said to have secured a $4.25 billion syndicated loan, a rare such deal in a region where lending has dropped off since the conflict in Iran began.
 
Elsewhere, AstraZeneca and Jaguar Land Rover are among over 100 UK companies that will be able to bring in workers on a fast-track visa as the government tries to capitalize on US President Trump’s immigration crackdown.
Some notable rating changes include Technip Energies cut to neutral at JPMorgan and InPost cut to neutral at UBS. Elsewhere, Jefferies cut Hensoldt to hold, and Oddo BHF cut Alior to neutral.
