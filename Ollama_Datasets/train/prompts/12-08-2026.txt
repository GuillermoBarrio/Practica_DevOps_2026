Subject: Before the European Bell: Stocks Need a New Reason to Rally
Before the European Bell: Stocks Need a New Reason to Rally
By Levin Stamm
08/12/2026 01:40:37 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- European stocks are headed for a muted open, continuing a subdued week as investors await fresh catalysts after a strong earnings season. Focus will later turn to US inflation data.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures were steady at 7:29 a.m. CET, while FTSE 100 futures fell 0.2%. The Stoxx 600 has gained for seven days in a row, its longest up streak since May 2025, coming out of the “Liberation Day” selloff.
Asian equities gained, buoyed by strong earnings reports from US tech names such as CoreWeave Inc. or Super Micro Computer Inc. Contracts for the Nasdaq 100 also tick higher.
Markets at a Glance:
 
Source: Bloomberg
Among the European season’s few remaining earnings, E.On reported adjusted Ebitda for the first half-year that met the average analyst estimate. Vestas Wind Systems lifted its profit guidance for the year after a jump in orders for its wind turbines. The Danish company said it expects a profit margin of between 7% to 9% for the full year, up from 6% and 8% previously.
 
In economic data, July’s US consumer price index reading — expected at 2:30 p.m. CET — should offer clues on the Federal Reserve’s future rate path, after Friday’s weak July jobs report tempered expectations for tighter policy, which had grown after some ratesetters dissented from the Fed’s recent decision to hold steady. Economists surveyed by Bloomberg expect prices paid by US consumers to rise 0.1%, following the prior month’s first decline in six years.
 
Separately, the Bank of France expects the country’s economy to maintain its momentum this quarter and gross domestic product to rise 0.2%, thanks to growth in key sectors and cooler inflation.
Such data points are likely to determine much of the price action in European stock markets in the next few weeks. Barclays strategists including Emmanuel Makonga note Europe’s momentum trade unwind is by now the second-worst since 2001 as investors have rushed out of crowded positions. 
 
Source: Barclays
Meanwhile, Bloomberg Opinion’s John Authers notes in his latest column that Europe, and Germany in particular, may currently be underappreciated by investors as evidenced by a supportive macroeconomic backdrop and strong earnings as well as a gap in valuations.
In geopolitics, Pakistan said the US and Iran are “close to some sort of arrangement” over the Strait of Hormuz, even after both sides appeared to toughen their positions in the long-deadlocked negotiations. Brent crude is up 0.4%, trading at just under $90 per barrel.
 
Some notable rating changes include Akzo Nobel and SBM Offshore being raised to overweight at Barclays. Elsewhere, Melrose Industries was lifted to buy at Kepler Cheuvreux.
