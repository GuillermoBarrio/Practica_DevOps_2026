Subject: Before the European Bell: Bulls Return After Valuation Pullback
Before the European Bell: Bulls Return After Valuation Pullback
By Farah Elbahrawy and Bre Bradham
07/31/2026 01:37:14 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- European stocks are poised to end the week higher, as sentiment rebounds after a sharp valuation pullback in this year’s standout AI trades.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures rose 0.8% at 7:25 a.m. CET, with contracts on the FTSE 100 up 0.5%. Nasdaq 100 futures led gains in the US after the underlying benchmark jumped 3.4% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index soared 8.2%.
In Asia, South Korean stocks surged by a record, rebounding on optimism that the unwinding of leveraged trades is nearing its end and the AI buildout will boost profits. SK Hynix surged close to the 30% daily limit, helped by a direct share purchase by SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won. In Japan, the BOJ kept its policy settings unchanged after authorities were said to have entered the currency market overnight to support the yen.
Renewed bullishness in markets is being driven in large part by results from US Big Tech firms and signs that, for some, AI spending is paying off — underpinned by strong demand across the sector. Amazon shares rallied in late trading after it reported cloud-computing revenue accelerated for a fifth straight quarter. On the flip side, Apple shares fell after component shortages weighed on the company’s sales forecast.
 
“Tighter financial conditions and capex angst weigh on Tech valuations, but earnings leadership remains largely intact. This may help providing a floor to the space,” said Barclays strategist Emmanuel Cau.
In Europe, Axa reported first half profit higher than the same period a year ago, while assessing the impact of wildfires currently affecting France and Spain. Credit Agricole reported better-than-expected results for the second quarter, as income rose at its French retail business and its asset management arm saw record profit. Holcim lifted its full-year guidance after reporting better-than-expected first-half profit. Brunello Cucinelli lifted its full-year guidance.
On the corporate front, Alimentation Couche-Tard said it agreed to buy a controlling stake in Polish retailer Żabka Group SA for about 32.6 billion zloty ($8.7 billion) to expand its retail footprint in central and eastern Europe. Meanwhile, Czech billionaire Michal Strnad has purchased about 14% of Pirelli from Chinese state-owned Sinochem, in a move that could help ease pressure over Beijing’s role in the Italian tiremaker.
Elsewhere, investors are monitoring developments about Leopold Aschenbrenner’s hedge fund Situational Awareness as its assets slumped to about $10 billion after Ken Griffin’s Citadel stepped in to buy the bulk of its public stock bets. Aschenbrenner’s fund is said to have began liquidating some of its equity positions after suffering losses in the AI stock rout in recent weeks, and this week’s sales have repaid the banks for the leverage Situational Awareness used to juice its bets.
JPMorgan strategists including Khuram Chaudhry said the quality style “has yet to meaningfully rotate, leaving the trade with ample room to run.” They’re long quality and “short junk” as equity sentiment had slipped close to negative and as money supply growth peaks, among other reasons, they wrote in a note.
 
On the economic front, July inflation figures due at 11 a.m. CET will provide the ECB with fresh insight ahead of its September meeting. Consumer prices likely rose 2.9% from a year ago.
The MSCI Europe Index is set for 14% EPS growth in the second quarter, beating analysts’ expectations of 11.5% ahead of the season.
