Subject: Before the European Bell: Big Bank Beats Offset Oil Rise
Before the European Bell: Big Bank Beats Offset Oil Rise
By Farah Elbahrawy and Neil Campling
07/29/2026 01:36:38 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- European stock futures are trimming early losses as strong earnings beats from banks are helping to offset pressure from higher oil prices and AI spending fears.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures were down 0.3% at 7:25 a.m. CET, while contracts on the FTSE 100 were 0.2% lower. Brent crude surged after the US said it intercepted an Iranian attack on military bases, ending a dayslong pause in fighting.
In Asia, SK Hynix shares tumbled as much as 20% after earmarking at least $31 billion for capital spending this year, adding to concerns that tech companies are overinvesting in AI. It sent the Kospi down as much as 13%.
 
The AI trade has come under even more scrutiny this week as investors question whether massive spending across the ecosystem will pay off. In Europe, perceived AI laggards are outperforming winners by a record margin in July. The focus will turn to spending figures from Meta and Microsoft which report after the US close later.
 
Over 50 companies on the Stoxx Europe 600 are reporting results Wednesday. The banks are a bright spot, as Standard Chartered announced a $1 billion share buyback as it reported second-quarter earnings that beat expectations. 
UBS profit also surpassed estimates and it announced a new $3 billion share buyback program to run until mid-2027. Deutsche Bank’s FIC sales & trading revenue for the second quarter beat the average analyst estimate, and the German bank announced a a new share buyback of €500 million.
Other highlights include Kering, whose ADRs surged after reporting that Gucci sales fell less than expected in the second quarter, suggesting efforts to revive the brand are starting to bear fruit. EssilorLuxottica said revenue from the Ray-Ban owner’s category-leading smartglasses nearly doubled in the second quarter. Meanwhile, Rio Tinto reported first-half underlying profit that beat the average analyst estimate, with strong copper earnings.
HSBC strategist Duncan Toms sees reasons to be optimistic about European equities in the second half. “Top down macro expectations could prove too pessimistic, particularly if higher policy uncertainty and currently hawkish ECB rate pricing unwind,” he writes. “Bottom up, earnings expectations have been more resilient with Q2 expected to be the strongest second quarter in three years.”
 
Out of the MSCI Europe companies that have reported so far this season, 47% have beaten expectations while 31% have missed. 
The attention will turn to the Federal Reserve after the close. It’s expected to hold interest rates steady but market participants are eyeing the possibility of a surprise hike as patience with high inflation wears thin.
