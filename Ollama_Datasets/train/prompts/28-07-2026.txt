Subject: Before the European Bell: Earnings Accelerate as Chips Retreat
Before the European Bell: Earnings Accelerate as Chips Retreat
By Neil Campling
07/28/2026 01:32:57 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- Futures point to a quiet open in Europe after a range-bound US session, while the rotation beneath the surface and the unwind of crowded trades continue to put chip stocks in the headlines.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures are 0.1% lower at 7:27 a.m. CET, while contracts on the FTSE 100 are down 0.3%. Asian markets are weak with the tech sector dragging on indexes in Japan, South Korea and Taiwan, reflecting a weak handover from Wall Street. Futures are pointing to a negative start in the US later, with Nasdaq contracts leading the decline.
Earnings continue at pace and the scorecard from Monday night for marque European names are a mixed bag. LVMH’s ADRs slid after the company’s key fashion and leather goods unit had organic sales rise 1% last quarter, missing analysts’ estimate of 1.52%. Elsewhere in fashion, Vinted has created a formidable marketplace that is eating into the sales of fashion and luxury houses, with the secondhand market expected to grow two to three times faster than the firsthand one from 2025 to 2027.
 
Philips raised its profitability expectations for 2026 after a US tariff refund bolstered earnings in the second quarter. Michelin reaffirmed full-year guidance as the tiremaker has so far avoided raw-material shortages considered manageable for now.
Among early Tuesday results, Safran raised its full-year guidance after strong demand for civil engine parts propelled its margin to a record. In contrast, Mercedes-Benz lowered its full-year guidance, citing weaker demand in China, where a prolonged property slump is undermining consumer sentiment and curbing demand for luxury vehicles.
More broadly, the increasingly interconnected web of dependencies between technology manufacturers and AI startups continues to dominate global sentiment. The risk of these “circular” deals was highlighted in a Bloomberg Technology explainer earlier this year, updated overnight here. 
The recent market pullback has been enough to erase the sell signal which was driven by sentiment and positioning, according to HSBC strategist Max Kettner, who says “there is good reason for the strength in risk assets.” He cites a solid start to the second-quarter reporting season, a reversal in cuts to US GDP forecasts and lower equity valuations, among other factors. Kettner previously went big on stocks when rivals blinked.  
Elsewhere, France and Spain are studying ways to pump money into regions hit by massive wildfires. Record-breaking heat and drought have fueled the worst start ever to the global wildfire season, with climate scientists warning that a developing El Niño could bring even harsher conditions later this summer. Meanwhile, Italy approved a temporary tax cut on diesel fuel through Aug. 6 to cushion the impact of surging energy prices.
There was some positive news for UK consumers and the Bank of England ahead of the interest rate decision later this week, with food inflation falling for a sixth straight month to 2.2% in July, according to British Retail Consortium data. 
 
 
Brent crude oil fell toward $87 a barrel following a 8.7% tumble on Monday, after President Trump said that the US and Iran were engaged in talks to try to end the Middle East conflict. Meanwhile, negotiators from Iran and Oman are said to be trying to reach an agreement to restart shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.
In politics, Labour MPs warned Andy Burnham he will lose the support of his governing party if he approaches welfare reform with a focus on “punitive” cuts. Seperately, Burnham ruled out any changes to the UK’s stamp duty tax on property purchases in his government’s first budget in the fall.

### FUENTE 2: FIVE THINGS:

07/28/2026 01:16:00 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) 
•	The Kospi slumped as much as 10.7%, heading for the worst session since early March as both Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix slid more than 12%. Korea Exchange triggered a circuit breaker for the benchmark, marking its eighth such halt this year. The Kosdaq also slid, set for the lowest close since mid-April. A $570 billion rout in just a little over a month has flipped Hynix from one of the world’s hottest AI trades to one of the biggest portfolio question marks.
•	Semiconductor stocks led declines in Asian equities with both the Nikkei and the Taiex dropping ~4%. Hang Seng and H-shares outperformed. Nasdaq-100 futures fell 0.9%. Nvidia is working on a fresh round of AI deals worth more than $750 billion, accelerating investments that skeptics have warned are artificially inflating demand and valuations across the industry. Meanwhile, Taiwanese prosecutors detained an Nvidia employee and searched the company’s offices as part of a widening probe into the alleged smuggling of AI accelerators into China, according to people familiar with the matter.
•	Oil extended declines, with Brent October contracts dropping below $85/bbl. President Trump said the US and Iran were engaged in diplomatic talks to end the Middle East conflict, but warned the two sides would return to fighting if a deal isn’t reached. Negotiators from Iran and Oman are trying to reach an agreement to restart shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, according to people familiar with the matter.
•	Crypto currencies declined, with Bitcoin dropping to ~$63,300. Precious metals also slid with gold falling to near $4,040/ounce. Interest-rate swaps imply about a 40% chance of a quarter-point increase by the Fed this week.
•	Mercedes-Benz cut its full-year guidance, citing weaker demand in China, where a prolonged property slump is undermining consumer sentiment and curbing demand for luxury vehicles. Meanwhile, France and Spain are studying ways to pump money into regions hit by massive wildfires over the past week even before the conflagrations are fully contained.
