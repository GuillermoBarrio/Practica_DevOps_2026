In economic data, July’s UK consumer prices — to be released at 8 a.m. CET — are expected to show inflation accelerating for the first time in four months, with the median economist estimate in a Bloomberg survey predicting a 2.9% year-on-year jump. That will be followed some hours later by final euro zone CPI numbers for July.
 
An even hotter print could fuel concerns that inflation across Europe will remain stubbornly above 2%, reinforcing expectations for a hawkish European Central Bank. Borrowing costs climbing to uncomfortable levels offer another reason to keep equity-market euphoria in check.
After Wednesday’s European market close, the Federal Reserve is expected to release minutes of its late-July meeting at which policymakers voted 9-3 to keep rates unchanged.
On a brighter note, the backdrop for European equities has grown increasingly supportive, with low volatility, improving earnings revisions and a steady stream of positive economic surprises. That marks a sharp shift in investor sentiment, with Europe until recently bashed for lacking the tech firepower to fully participate in the global artificial-intelligence investment boom.
 
The region’s stock-market calm has reached a rare extreme, Bloomberg’s Christian Dass writes, with trailing five-day realized volatility on the Stoxx Europe 600 in the bottom 0.3% of observations, as of Monday’s close. 
Meanwhile, the ratio of European earnings upgrades to downgrades is at its highest in data going back to early 2024, according to Deutsche Bank strategists. Plus, with the number of positive economic surprises mounting, the share of investors expecting a European recession has slumped, according to Bank of America’s latest fund manager survey.
