Before the European Bell: Winners From AI Spending in Spotlight
By Farah Elbahrawy
08/14/2026 01:36:22 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- European stocks were set to eke out a small gain at the open after a rally in the US. The AI trade is the driving force behind the market, but focus in Europe extends beyond the marquee tech names.
Euro Stoxx 50 and FTSE 100 futures gained 0.3% at 7:25 a.m. CET. Contracts on the S&P 500 were muted after the underlying benchmark hit a record Thursday, as further signs of cooling inflation dampened expectations for a Federal Reserve interest-rate hike next month. Asian stocks advanced on Friday, with the Kospi taking its weekly gain to nearly 11%.
Markets at a Glance:
 
Source: Bloomberg
Developments in the AI space continue to shape the narrative as the trade comes back in favor. Bloomberg reported OpenAI is on track to generate annualized revenue of more than $40 billion based on its current performance, roughly doubling its run rate from the end of 2025 and bolstering the company’s plans for a Wall Street debut. 
Meanwhile, AMD raised $4.75 billion in the chipmaker’s biggest-ever US dollar bond offering, adding to a wave of debt tied to the artificial intelligence boom.
A Morgan Stanley team led by Chief European Equity Strategist Marina Zavolock said “we continue to recommend buying the dip in AI capex winners, fading the recent bottom momentum squeeze and reiterate that market breadth has likely peaked for now.”
 
This has been a relatively lackluster week for European stocks amid lower summer volumes and a lack of fresh catalysts. Still, AI winners are among the key leaders on the Stoxx 600 this week, including ASML, Nokia and Siemens Energy. The European stock market is packed with more than enough AI winners to offset its lack of technology shares, writes Bloomberg Senior Strategist Michael Msika.
It’s also been supportive for European flows. Citigroup analysts said European equity funds had a net inflow of $1.2 billion in the week through August 12, which mainly went into regional and Switzerland funds.
Meanwhile, defense shares have been rebounding this quarter. The sector will be closely watched on Friday as the Trump administration is applying a 100% tariff on imports of unmanned aircraft systems and their components. Tariffs on drones from several economies that struck trade deals with Trump, like the EU and Switzerland, will be 15%, so long as all hardware and technology originates from those places and the US. The UK will be subject to a 10% tariff on its drones and components. 
 
Elsewhere, UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham and his chancellor of the exchequer, John Healey, are expected to focus on cutting business rates for smaller companies in their first budget in October, according to people familiar with their plans. The prime minister has publicly suggested he would support paying for that by shifting taxes onto larger firms with bigger footprints, though hitting some bigger companies might cause them to pass prices onto consumers.
