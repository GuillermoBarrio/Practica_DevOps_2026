Over the weekend, more ships came under attack in the Strait of Hormuz as talks to end the war in the Middle East remain stalled and the US readies new economic measures to force Iran to capitulate. A quieter week on the corporate front shifts focus to macro data. This includes flash Eurozone PMIs Friday — the last before the September ECB meeting — and Germany’s ZEW reading Tuesday. Key questions for the week ahead, as posed in the Week in Review, include: Can Europe’s earnings momentum survive the end of reporting season? Will oil finally break the equity market’s resilience? And can the Stoxx 600 turn consolidation into another breakout?
JPMorgan strategist Mislav Matejka highlights Europe is attempting to move from a model targeting efficiency and economies of scale toward a greater focus on security, resiliency, and strategic autonomy. The implication for markets is a longer lasting, policy-supported capex cycle that — relative to the US — starts from deeper under-investment in defence readiness, grid build, and industrial capacity, so the catch up could be meaningful.
 
The heatwave in Europe continues to generate headlines. Belgian firefighters battled a large wildfire near the German border on Sunday, and “horrific” conditions grip UK hospitals unprepared for the heat. Staying in Britain, the Ministry of Defense is stepping-up scrutiny of its supply chain after discovering some naval drones transmitted signals to China.
Germany’s pension system is about to undergo major reform and has money managers racing to have new products ready. The rise in France’s unemployment rate last quarter was a result of the war in the Middle East and the heat waves that have struck the country, according to Labor Minister Jean-Pierre Farandou.
In AI, Alibaba’s open-weight models have accumulated more than 3 billion global downloads in the past six months, eclipsing Meta, Alphabet. and domestic peers to become the world’s No. 1 AI model. And while the technology may be assumed to be less prevalent in Europe than the US, company executives in the region mentioned AI more than four times on average on post-earnings conference calls this season — an all-time peak and above the average of 0.5 mentions since 2016. 
In other assets, bond traders fear around $70 billion in phantom liabilities don’t appear on major AI companies’ balance sheets, but could materialize at the worst possible time. Jane Street posted roughly $15 billion of losses in July — its first monthly slump in about a decade.
