Before the European Bell: Earnings Push Stocks to Records
By Levin Stamm and Neil Campling
08/05/2026 01:39:26 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- European stocks are set for fresh record highs after a tech-driven rally in the US, with strong earnings momentum reinforcing the case for the region’s equities.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures are 0.4% higher at 7:26 a.m. CET, while FTSE 100 futures are up 0.2%. Asian equities rallied, led by chip stocks.
Contracts for the S&P 500 are also up after the underlying benchmark hit a record high Tuesday. Nasdaq 100 futures rose, even as SpaceX shares tumbled following higher-than-expected AI spending in the firm’s inaugural earnings report. US chipmaker AMD also slumped in late trading after giving an underwhelming sales outlook.
Markets at a Glance
 
Source: Bloomberg
Among European key earnings that will shape the day, Novo Nordisk shares will likely follow their ADRs lower at the open, after sales of the new Wegovy weight-loss pill failed to top analysts’ expectations, offsetting the pharma company’s second forecast raise this year. 
AMD is a fresh disappointment for the chip sector which was just coming back to life after a sharp selloff. In Europe, German chipmaker Infineon has the biggest share-price gain on the Euro Stoxx 50 this year, but has already slumped 28% from an all time high.
Infineon forecast revenue in the current quarter that beat analysts’ estimates as the German chipmaker profits from rising demand for its data-center chips and price increases.
 
Barclays strategists led by Emmanuel Cau say the “momentum washout has reduced AI froth, allowing sound earnings fundamentals to prevail again.” He added diversification appetite, strong nominal growth as well as lower oil prices should act in favor for Europe, value and cyclicals.
That’s particularly true as economic momentum in Europe is outpacing a comparable gauge in the US for the first time since before the Iran war. A Citigroup index is at a three-year high, with a positive reading indicating data releases are coming in stronger than expected. 
 
Meanwhile, Brent crude edged lower to trade at $79 per barrel, extending its decline for a third day, on improved prospects for a US-Iran pace deal that was nurtured by news of a call between President Donald Trump Qatar’s Emir Sheikh Tamim Bin Hamad Al-Thani to discuss efforts to de-escalate US-Iran tensions.
