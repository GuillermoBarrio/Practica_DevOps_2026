Before the European Bell: AI Revival Confronts Earnings Hurdle
By Farah Elbahrawy
08/13/2026 01:33:45 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- European stocks are set for a positive open after a strong handover from Wall Street. The artificial intelligence trade is attempting a comeback, but earnings disappointments could threaten it.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures gained 0.5% at 7:25 a.m. CET, while FTSE 100 futures rose 0.3%. Contracts on the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 pointed to further gains.
Markets at a Glance:
 
Source: Bloomberg
In Asia, a revival in the global AI trade pushed South Korean stocks toward a technical bull market, marking a dramatic turnaround from last month’s historic rout. The Kospi gained as much as 4.8%, led by memory chipmakers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix.
A UBS basket of European AI winners has rallied this month, rebounding from a record underperformance in July relative to stocks seen as most at risk from the technology.
 
The trade will be tested on Thursday after overnight earnings from key AI plays in the US disappointed. Cerebras Systems slid in late trading after projecting slower growth than some investors anticipated. There are lingering questions about its hardware business, which was eclipsed by its cloud computing service.
Cisco Systems fell after forecasting $7.5 billion in sales tied to the AI data center boom this fiscal year. The figures were seen as conservative, especially for the firm facing high expectations that it will profit from the worldwide build-out of data centers powering AI systems. Europe’s network-equipment companies Nokia and Ericsson could be active after the report.
Meanwhile, harsh weather conditions are still in focus. Europe’s fifth heat wave of the summer is set to peak Thursday, the latest in a string of extreme weather events that have strained health systems and energy networks across the region. A series of high-pressure heat domes has put the UK on track for its hottest ever summer.
Investors will be monitoring a slew of economic data due from the UK at 8 a.m. CET. Gross domestic product is expected to have climbed 0.4% in the second quarter, slightly down from 0.6% growth during the first three months of the year. Spain will release final inflation figures for July an hour later. 
On the geopolitical front, Brent crude edged lower after six straight sessions of gains. Facing a shortfall of necessary munitions and wary of continuing an unpopular war, Trump and his top officials are returning to a familiar playbook with Iran: relying on a steady increase in economic sanctions and a naval blockade to stifle oil exports.

(Bloomberg) 
•	The yen strengthened modestly and Japanese bond yields rose slightly on a report that Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi’s government is supportive of a near-term rate hike by the Bank of Japan. Fears about yen weakness at the central bank and the government’s desire to strengthen the impact of recent FX intervention are aligning them on the need to raise rates, according to people familiar with the matter.
•	Treasury yields are down about two basis points across the curve. Short-end rates are falling in Australia after RBA comments that falling house prices may reduce the role of monetary policy, and there’s a similar move in New Zealand on a drop in inflation expectations. The US yesterday sold 10-year bonds at the highest yield since 2007 and has a 30-year sale on the slate today.
•	Tech stocks are rallying in Asia after benign US inflation and a decline in oil prices provided a positive setup for equities. The Kospi has jumped more than 20% in 10 days as the AI rally regained steam, while equities in Taiwan and Japan are also climbing. US stock index futures are edging up.
•	Base metals weakened, with copper slipping toward $14,000/ton and aluminum and nickel off too. Gold is holding around $4,400/oz. The Bank of Korea made its first gold-related investment for 13 years in the second quarter.
•	Cisco’s better-than-anticipated sales and forecast failed to hit high expectations, while chipmaker and cloud computing firm Cerebras Systems’ growth projections fell short. Both fell in late trading. Anthropic is in talks to buy AI startup Decart AI for around $6 billion. The company makes software to reduce the cost of training AI models and focuses elsewhere on generative video.
