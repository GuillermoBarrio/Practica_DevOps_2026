Before the European Bell: Broader Leadership Starts to Emerge
By Farah Elbahrawy and Julien Ponthus
08/07/2026 01:32:46 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- European stock futures were muted as oil climbed, though regional benchmarks are still headed for a weekly gain as investors seek strong earnings growth at enticing valuations.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures were 0.1% lower at 7:25 a.m. CET, while contracts on the FTSE 100 were down 0.2%. Brent crude climbed after a report that Iran attacked “hostile targets” in the Strait of Hormuz, with Tehran seeking to bar US and Israeli ships from the critical waterway in a deal with Oman.
Asian stocks were mixed and US stock futures were flat after a weak session on Wall Street. Traders are awaiting US job data for clues on the outlook for interest rates. Bloomberg Economics expects nonfarm payrolls increased at a modest pace and the unemployment rate edged up in July.

•	Alphabet sold $25 billion in investment-grade bonds having attracted $115 billion in orders for the sale, one of the largest books so far for AI-related debt. UK-based AI cloud platform Nscale is telling potential investors it has $51 billion in contracted revenue ahead of a planned US IPO.
•	Market-maker Jane Street is negotiating with a group of investors, including Pimco, on a private credit deal to rework its $11 billion debt pile. Results from publicly-traded private credit firms, meanwhile, have suggested a focus on trimming bad investments or lowering leverage six months on from the industry being engulfed by concerns about its stability.
•	Apollo has agreed to buy UK budget airline EasyJet for £5.7 billion ($7.7 billion) after rival suitor Castlelake pulled out of the process. EasyJet shares closed below the level of Apollo’s bid on Thursday and have consistently been below the escalating offer prices on doubts about the regulatory hurdles the deal will face.
