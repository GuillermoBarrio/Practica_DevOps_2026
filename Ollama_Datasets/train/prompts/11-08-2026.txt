Before the European Bell: Positioning Shows a Clear Recovery
By Neil Campling
08/11/2026 01:41:55 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- Futures point to a lackluster start to cash trading in Europe, reflecting a subdued finish on Wall Street and a drifting session in Asia. Traders appear to have become used to the protracted uncertainty over progress in the Middle East. 
Euro Stoxx 50 futures and contracts on the FTSE 100 were steady as of 7:29 a.m. CET. US Treasury yields, gold and European natural gas prices have ticked higher.
Asian stocks are little-changed, with the Kospi leading the region, while Japan is closed for Mountain Day holiday although the Yen has continued to weaken, erasing half of its rally from recent interventions. Futures point to modest gains in the US later. Brent crude is holding on to Monday’s moves, while gold’s rally briefly pushed on through the $4,400 per ounce level. 
 
Source: Bloomberg
Citigroup strategists led by David Chew said “Europe remains one of the clearest positioning recovery stories globally.” Activity was dominated by new long accumulation last week, lifting Euro Stoxx 50 and FTSE positioning toward moderately bullish levels.
 
Goldman Sachs strategist Sharon Bell debunks a number of myths about European equities in a note to clients; including that Europe has no growth, has low ROE, always underperforms, that China competition is a huge negative, higher energy prices hit EPS, and the region is cheap because of low growth. Bell also highlights European equities are seeing the best inflows in 10-years excluding 2021.
On the macro front, Bloomberg Economics’ Global GDP Tracker points to the return of growth momentum, running at an annualized quarterly rate of 4% in July, up from 3.7% previously and above its long-run average of 3.6%. The yuan is possibly 21% undervalued in multilateral and inflation-adjusted terms, based on Bank for International Settlements and IMF data, according to Bloomberg Intelligence.
 
President Trump made sweeping new demands on Iran after Tehran reiterated requests for reparations as part of talks to wind down the conflict, dimming hopes of a quick deal that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Seperately, Trump extended a waiver allowing foreign ships to transport oil and other commodities around the US for 90 more days, albeit with new limits. A drone attack on an oil refinery in western Libya destroyed a storage tank, putting oil facilities at risk and potentially aggravating fuel shortages in the OPEC nation.
In other assets, gold advanced to a two-month high above $4,400 an ounce, with technical buying spurred by the metal’s ascent through the 100-day moving average on Monday. Global gas turbine orders hit a record in the April-to-June quarter on surging growth in power demand, according to JPMorgan. The recent decline in the US dollar hedge ratio is reminiscent of the drop before Trump’s sweeping tariffs in April 2025, which left the US currency vulnerable to a selloff, according to ING. 
In finance, European private equity firm Exponent has closed a €750 million ($866 million) continuation fund that extends its investment in H&MV Engineering, which works on high-voltage projects and is expanding in the US. UBS is seeking to overturn an arbitration award of deferred compensation to Credit Suisse Group’s former global co-head of foreign exchange and precious metals trading.
