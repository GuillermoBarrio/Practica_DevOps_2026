Before the European Bell: Sweet Spot Endures on Strong Earnings
By Farah Elbahrawy and Neil Campling
08/04/2026 01:33:11 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- European stocks are on track for a positive open, adding to a rally that has already pushed regional indexes to fresh records. Strong earnings, resilient economic data and supportive positioning continue to give investors plenty of reasons to stay optimistic.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures are 0.3% higher at 7:25 a.m. CET after the underlying benchmark closed at a record the prior session, while FTSE 100 futures are up 0.2%. In Asia, stocks are under pressure as concerns about the AI trade linger, with TSMC and Samsung weighing the most on the MSCI Asia Pacific.
Markets at a Glance
 
Source: Bloomberg
Meanwhile, Brent crude is edging higher after its biggest drop in a week. Trump said his latest offer of talks is Tehran’s “last chance” and that he expects a full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz.
Among the key earnings before Europe’s open on Tuesday, HSBC announced a fresh stock buyback and raised its cost-cutting target as it reported second-quarter earnings that beat estimates. Lufthansa warned of heightened uncertainty for its full-year outlook as volatility in jet fuel prices and shortened booking cycles complicate efforts to boost profit.
 
While recent economic data have been encouraging, extreme weather in Europe this summer has complicated the situation due to wildfires and falling water levels. The Rhine River’s water levels have dropped to the lowest in nearly 150 years, threatening to disrupt a crucial route for transporting coal, fuel and industrial commodities across a large swath of the continent. 
 
Meanwhile, Europe is increasingly benefiting from a sharp decline in the US market’s free cash flow yield. That’s prompting interest in markets able to produce sustainable earnings growth without it coming at a cost to cash reserves, like Europe.
Citigroup strategists, including David Chew, say Europe stands out as the only major region experiencing a “meaningful improvement in risk appetite, supported by fresh long accumulation and favorable earnings delivery.” The team writes that positioning “remains constructive rather than euphoric.”
 
The earnings season in the region has been strong, with companies on track to post second-quarter EPS growth of more than 14% year-over-year, beating an estimated 11.5%. Out of the companies that have reported, 56% have beaten expectations while 25% of them have missed.

### FUENTE 2: FIVE THINGS:
--- INICIO ---
08/04/2026 01:19:56 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) 
•	S&P 500 futures climbed to the cusp of a record and Nasdaq-100 futures are in the green, pointing to an extension of tech-driven rallies. Palantir hiked revenue and profit forecasts for the year to fuel AI enthusiasm, saying commercial demand for its data analytics tools is “otherworldly.” The Kospi experienced more volatility, swinging between gains and losses, with tech stocks more broadly slipping in Asia.
•	European stock futures are higher too, with a wave of earnings dropping. HSBC’s profit topped expectations and it reinstated its share buyback program, which was suspended when it acquired Hang Seng Bank. German carrier Lufthansa’s earnings fell on higher fuel costs, echoing results from long-haul peers. Oil major BP, car parts manufacturer Continental and German pharmaceuticals and crop science giant Bayer are all on the slate too.
•	The yen is snapping its winning run following further intervention speculation in the wake of last week’s coordinated US-Japan action. That’s putting the prospect of whether the yen can strengthen beyond 155 per dollar in focus. Meanwhile, Japanese government bonds were rattled by weak demand at a 10-year auction.
•	Brent is rising and sitting above $85/bbl after President Trump said his latest offer of talks with Iran is a “last chance.” Iran denied it was negotiating with the US, but said talks with Oman on getting more ships moving through the Strait of Hormuz were progressing.
•	Water levels in the Rhine River dropped to the lowest in nearly 150 years as hot, dry weather grips Europe. The water level at the key Kaub choke point fell to the lowest since records began in 1880. Freight rates are soaring as barges reduce cargo to navigate through the shallow waters, with the cost of shipping diesel from Rotterdam to Karlsruhe climbing to the loftiest point since Bloomberg started tracking the data in 2009.
