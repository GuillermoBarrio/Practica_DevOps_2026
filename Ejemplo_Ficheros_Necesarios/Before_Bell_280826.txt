Before the European Bell: Summer Lull Faces Final Big Test
By Farah Elbahrawy
08/28/2026 01:33:17 [BFW]
(Bloomberg) -- European stocks need a positive session to snap their longest weekly losing streak since March, while a busy slate of economic data and Fed Chair Warsh’s highly anticipated speech at Jackson Hole could provide direction.
Euro Stoxx 50 futures gained 0.3% at 7:25 a.m. CET, while FTSE 100 contracts rose 0.2%. US equity index futures were steady after Nvidia added $442 billion to its market value on Thursday, in the second-largest gain by any stock over one day in history. Asian stocks climbed for a fourth day.
Markets at a Glance:
 
Bloomberg
All focus will turn to Kevin Warsh’s first major speech as chairman of the Fed, which has become an unexpected trial of his slimmed-down communications style. Central bank watchers at JPMorgan, Apollo Global Management and Morgan Stanley say Warsh has a chance to convince the market that managing inflation is his priority. His speech is set for 4 p.m. CET.
Friday’s price action is set to determine the weekly direction for the Stoxx 600, which had slid 0.4% through Thursday. European stock funds had outflows of about $100 million in the week through August 26, according to a Citigroup note citing EPFR Global data.
“The risk-reward landscape is changing and caution against chasing the high beta and high volatility rallies here,” write JPMorgan quant strategists including Khuram Chaudhry. Their analysis of market concentration points to long quality and short risk, the team writes in a note.
 
It’s a busy day for economic data in the region. French and Spanish flash inflation numbers are predicted to show yet another uptick in price pressures in August. Euro-area data will only be revealed the following week, with the releases set to help shape the European Central Bank’s Sept. 10 rate decision.
French assets will be in focus anew after bank stocks dragged the CAC 40 lower on Thursday ahead of the country’s first presidential election debate. The discussion was largely consumed by bickering among the candidates on how to deal with the country’s swelling debt, including a dispute over a far-left proposal to simply cancel a portion of it. Fitch Ratings is also scheduled to review its sovereign credit rating of France on Friday.
 
Brent crude was about steady on Friday morning, trading just below $90 a barrel, as the likelihood of a US-Iran deal to fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz faded. The top US commander for the Middle East said in a new video that American forces have cleared Iranian mines from shipping lanes in the strait, though some allies have been skeptical of Washington’s claims.
Among key corporate news in Europe, Shell is said to have ended years-long talks with Poland’s Unimot SA over selling its stake in Germany’s Russia-linked Schwedt refinery. Meanwhile, Assicurazioni Generali said it is willing to consider the takeover offer for its wealth management unit Banca Generali from Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena.
